## Scenario 1 — EO 14154 / BOEM COP Approval Freeze

### Mechanism

Two stacked executive instruments control this risk channel:

1. **Presidential Memorandum of January 20, 2025, "Temporary Withdrawal of All Areas on the Outer Continental Shelf from Offshore Wind Leasing and Review of the Federal Government's Leasing and Permitting Practices for Wind Projects."** Issued under §12(a) of the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act (43 U.S.C. §1341(a)), which gives the President plenary authority to withdraw unleased OCS lands. The memo (a) withdraws all unleased OCS areas from wind leasing indefinitely, and (b) directs Interior, in consultation with the AG, to conduct a "comprehensive review" of existing leases and permits, with authority to recommend "termination or amendment."
2. **Executive Order 14154, "Unleashing American Energy," January 20, 2025.** Directs all agencies to pause disbursement of IRA/IIJA funds, review regulations that "burden" domestic energy production, and rescind specific Biden-era guidance. §7 specifically targets wind. Combined effect: BOEM, BSEE, NMFS, USACE, and EPA are instructed to slow-roll any approvals that "subsidize" or "facilitate" offshore wind absent specific White House sign-off.

The pathway to **stop a Construction & Operations Plan (COP) approval** runs through BOEM's regulations at **30 C.F.R. Part 585, subpart F** (§§585.620–.638). BOEM has discretion to "disapprove" or require modification at §585.628. The administration's lever is delay-by-review rather than formal disapproval, which would create cleaner APA challenge surface.

### Current status as of late May 2026

**[VERIFIED through early 2025; INFERRED for 2025–2026 trajectory]**

- The January 20, 2025 PM is in force. **No new commercial OCS wind lease auctions have been held since Inflation Reduction Act-era Gulf of Mexico Lease Sale 1 (Aug 2023) and the Central Atlantic Lease Sale (Aug 2024).** The Oregon and Gulf of Maine sales that were on BOEM's 2024 schedule were either cancelled or have not been re-noticed.
- **COP-level decisions** for projects with executed leases (which the PM cannot legally withdraw without compensation) are the live battleground. The administration's posture has been:
  - Issued Records of Decision are *not* being formally rescinded (Vineyard Wind 1, South Fork, Revolution Wind, CVOW, Empire Wind 1 already had RODs pre-2025).
  - But **Notices to Proceed**, **incidental take authorizations**, and **USACE §10/§404 permits** for downstream phases are being slow-walked.
- **Empire Wind 1 stop-work order, April 16, 2025.** Interior Secretary Burgum directed BOEM to halt construction activities pending additional review; this was lifted approximately May 19, 2025 after negotiation with New York State (widely reported as linked to NY's posture on a natural-gas pipeline). This episode is the most concrete evidence of the administration's willingness to use mid-construction halt authority. **[VERIFIED]**
- **Atlantic Shores South:** Shell wrote down its ~$1B stake in late 2024/early 2025; EPA EAB remanded the project's PSD/Title V air permit (see Scenario 5). The project is effectively in suspended animation as of May 2026. **[VERIFIED through 2025]**
- **Contract library milestone "BOEM Atlantic Permitting Pause Lift"** — as of late May 2026 the author has **no verified evidence this has occurred**. Base case: still in force.

### Historical precedent (base rates)

- **Cape Wind (2001–2017):** federal permits issued, then died from cumulative state PPA and litigation drag. Demonstrates that administration *opposition* is sufficient to kill a project even without formal denial.
- **Vineyard Wind 1 (2019 supplemental EIS pause under Trump 1.0):** BOEM under Sec. Bernhardt paused the FEIS in Aug 2019 ostensibly for cumulative-impacts analysis. The pause lasted ~18 months; project was approved May 2021 under Biden. **Base rate: a determined administration can add 12–24 months to a COP timeline via "supplemental analysis" with low litigation risk.**
- **Keystone XL (2015, 2017, 2021):** demonstrates that executive permit reversal survives APA review if the agency builds a defensible record.
- **Lava Ridge wind (onshore, Idaho)**, June 2025 PM withdrawing the ROD: precedent for *formal* ROD rescission, though onshore BLM not BOEM. **[VERIFIED early-mid 2025]**

Watch for "Notice of Availability of Final Environmental Impact Statement" filings. **As of KB cutoff, cadence has dropped to near-zero.**
3. **Interior Secretary's Section-by-Section review report** mandated by the Jan 20, 2025 PM. Originally due within 90 days (i.e., ~April 20, 2025). **[UNKNOWN whether published]** — its release is a high-information event.
4. **Empire Wind 2 / NY OREC re-procurement status** — NYSERDA solicitations: https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/All-Programs/Offshore-Wind. Re-bid activity signals state willingness to bridge federal hostility.
5. **Litigation docket: *American Clean Power Association v. Department of the Interior*** (or analogous trade-association suit challenging the PM). The author is **not certain** such a suit has been filed as of May 2026; check PACER and the ACP press room (https://cleanpower.org/news/).

### Quantification

For a $4B+ project, the COP-freeze channel produces impact through **schedule slippage**, not direct cost. Rule of thumb:

- 6-month additional permitting delay on a project in late development: **3–6% of capex** in carrying costs, escalation, and contractor demob/remob.
- Full RODs-rescission (low-probability, high-impact tail): **40–100% of capex** depending on cure rights and PPA termination triggers.
- Stop-work order mid-construction (Empire Wind precedent): **8–15% of capex** per 6-month halt, dominated by vessel standby ($150–300k/day for a WTIV) and financing carry.

**Probability over 18 months (through Nov 2027):**
- *Continued de facto COP freeze for projects not already under construction:* **70–85%**
- *Formal rescission of an executed lease or issued ROD (high-impact tail):* **8–15%** — limited by Takings Clause exposure
- *Mid-construction stop-work episode on at least one major project:* **35–50%** (Empire Wind shows this is a live tool)

## Scenario 2 — Section 232 Steel Tariffs on Monopile / Tower Steel

### Mechanism

Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962 (19 U.S.C. §1862) authorizes the President to adjust imports threatening national security. The 2018 steel investigation (Proclamation 9705, March 8, 2018) imposed a 25% global tariff on steel mill products listed in **HTS Chapter 72 and parts of 73**. Relevant codes for offshore wind:

- **HTS 7208.51** — hot-rolled non-alloy steel plate, >10mm thickness, not in coils. Core monopile and transition-piece input.
- **HTS 7208.52** — hot-rolled plate 4.75–10mm.
- **HTS 7305.11 / 7305.12 / 7305.19** — large-diameter line pipe and welded tube, used for tower sections and some monopile fabrications (welded variants).
- **HTS 7308.20** — towers and lattice masts (some imported finished towers fall here, though most are now domestically rolled).

**Trump 2.0 expansion: Proclamation 10896, February 10, 2025** ("Adjusting Imports of Steel into the United States"). Key changes vs. 2018 baseline:
- Re-imposed 25% on **all** countries, terminating the country-specific exemptions, TRQs, and absolute quotas previously granted to South Korea, Brazil, Argentina, EU, UK, Japan.
- Terminated the product-specific exclusion process administered by Commerce/BIS.
- Expanded coverage to additional "derivative" articles.

**[VERIFIED]** for Feb 2025 action. **[INFERRED]** — a subsequent **June 2025 doubling to 50%** was widely reported in trade press; the author's confidence is moderate. For risk-book purposes, treat the **operating tariff as 50% on the relevant HTS lines as of late May 2026**, with low confidence on the exact effective date.

### Current status as of late May 2026

- Steel tariff rate on HTS 7208.51 plate: **50%** ad valorem (assumed). No general country exclusions. No active product exclusion process at BIS.
- Domestic monopile fabrication capacity is constrained. EEW AOS Paulsboro, NJ is the principal US monopile facility, with capacity in the low double-digits of monopiles per year — well below what a single utility-scale project (60–100 monopiles) needs in a campaign year. Sif (Netherlands), Steelwind Nordenham (Germany), and Haizea (Spain) remain the marginal suppliers.
- **Inflation Reduction Act §45X advanced manufacturing PTC** for offshore wind components (10% of cost for offshore wind foundations, towers; specific per-watt rates for blades/nacelles) is in tension with the tariff stack — but §45X is a production credit *to the US manufacturer*, not a tariff offset for the developer importing a foreign monopile.

### Historical precedent

- **2018 §232 round:** developers (then mostly Vineyard Wind, South Fork) sought product exclusions for steel grades not produced domestically (e.g., specific thick plate >100mm). Exclusion grant rate for offshore-wind-specific petitions was below 50% and timelines stretched 6–9 months. Several offshore developers redesigned to use sub-100mm plate or sourced from Nucor/SSAB Americas where feasible.
- **§301 China tariffs (2018–present):** less directly relevant — almost no Chinese steel in the qualified offshore wind supply chain due to Buy America / domestic content posture.
- **Solar §201 (2018, extended 2022):** demonstrated that even with a tariff in place, the broader tax-credit stack can sustain deployment; but module costs are a smaller share of solar capex than monopile steel is of offshore wind capex.

### Leading indicators

1. **BIS Section 232 page**: https://www.bis.doc.gov/index.php/other-areas/office-of-technology-evaluation-ote/section-232-investigations. Watch for any reopened exclusion process.
2. **USITC HTS online**: https://hts.usitc.gov/ — verify current chapter-99 additional duty subheadings (9903.81.xx) attached to 7208.51.
3. **Federal Register Proclamations** issued under §232: search https://www.federalregister.gov/presidential-documents/proclamations.
4. **CBP rulings** on country-of-origin for monopiles fabricated in third countries from upstream slab: https://rulings.cbp.gov/. Origin shifting is the principal mitigation play; CBP origin rulings are the diagnostic.
5. **EEW AOS Paulsboro production rate** — proxy via NJ EDA filings and Ørsted/Equinor disclosures.

### Quantification

Steel content of a monopile foundation: ~1,500–2,500 metric tons of plate per unit, depending on water depth and turbine size. At $1,200–1,800/ton landed plate cost pre-tariff, a 50% tariff adds **$600–$2.25M per monopile**. For a 100-turbine project: **$60–$225M tariff incidence on monopiles alone**, plus tower steel (~150–250 tons/tower) adding **$10–$30M**.

**Expressed as % of $4B capex:**
- Base case (current 50% tariff, no exclusions): **2–6% of capex** in landed-cost uplift, of which some is absorbed by EPC contingency and some passes through to PPA price (if renegotiable).
- Tail case (tariff doubled again to 100% or expansion to currently-uncovered subheadings): **5–10% of capex**.

**Probability over 18 months:**
- *Tariff rate remains at or above 50% on relevant HTS lines:* **85–95%**
- *Reinstatement of meaningful product-exclusion process:* **10–20%** — politically disfavored
- *Further upward adjustment (>50%) or expansion to currently-uncovered derivatives:* **25–40%**

## Scenario 3 — Jones Act Installation-Vessel Bottleneck

### Mechanism

**46 U.S.C. §55102** ("Jones Act") prohibits the transportation of merchandise between US points by a non-US-built, non-US-flagged, non-US-owned vessel. The CBP-blessed workaround for offshore wind has been the **"feeder vessel" model**: a foreign-flag Wind Turbine Installation Vessel (WTIV) jacks up at the lease site (which is a US point only once a structure is bottomed-out on the OCS), while US-built feeder barges shuttle components from US ports. The legal foundation is a line of CBP rulings interpreting "merchandise transportation" — most notably **HQ H309186 (2020)** and successors.

Key vulnerabilities:
- The OCS becomes a "point in the United States" for Jones Act purposes once a wind structure is fixed (per **OCSLA §4(a)(1)** as amended by the 2021 NDAA §9503, Pub. L. 116-283). Pre-fixation, the lease site is *not* a US point and a foreign WTIV can in principle deliver directly.
- CBP can reverse its own rulings via a HQ letter ruling and a 60-day notice-and-comment under **19 C.F.R. §177.12**.
- **CBP Office of Trade enforcement actions** carry penalties up to the **value of the merchandise transported** under 46 U.S.C. §55102(c).

### Current status as of late May 2026

- **Charybdis (Dominion Energy / Keppel AmFELS, Brownsville TX):** First US-built, Jones-Act-compliant WTIV. Originally targeted 2023 delivery, slipped to late 2024/2025. **[VERIFIED through early 2025: sea trials underway; delivery imminent or just-completed.]** **[INFERRED]** that by late May 2026 Charybdis is in commercial service on CVOW. Day rate believed to be in the $200–300k/day range.
- **Maersk Supply / Empire State (Eneti / Cadeler joint vessel programs):** non-Jones-Act, available for feeder-supported installation.
- **Feeder barge fleet:** Foss Maritime, Great Lakes Dredge & Dock, and several specialist operators have built or chartered US-flag feeder tonnage. Bottleneck has shifted somewhat from WTIVs to **heavy-lift feeders capable of handling next-generation 14–18 MW nacelles**.
- **CBP enforcement posture under Trump 2.0:** The administration's general protectionist orientation makes a tightening of "feeder model" rulings plausible but **the author has no verified rescission of HQ H309186 or its successors as of KB cutoff**. This is a watch item.

### Historical precedent

- **2009 CBP ruling reversal episode:** CBP issued, then withdrew under industry pressure, a set of rulings that would have classified routine offshore oil-and-gas operations as Jones Act-violative. Demonstrates the agency's sensitivity to industry blowback.
- **2019 NDAA / 2021 NDAA OCS amendments:** Congress repeatedly clarified that the Jones Act applies to OCS renewable energy. Each iteration tightened scope.
- **Liftboat / lift-on-lift-off feeder model on Block Island Wind Farm (2016):** the original US offshore wind project used Fred. Olsen's Brave Tern (Marshall Islands flag) with US-flag feeders. CBP did not challenge. Established the operational precedent.

### Leading indicators

1. **CBP HQ rulings database**: https://rulings.cbp.gov/ — search "wind turbine installation" and "OCS." Watch for any new HQ ruling modifying H309186 line.
2. **Dominion 10-Q / 10-K disclosures on Charybdis utilization** — https://investors.dominionenergy.com/. Charter-out rate to third parties signals fleet tightness.
3. **MARAD Title XI loan guarantee applications** for new US-build WTIVs: https://www.maritime.dot.gov/. Pipeline of next-generation US-flag tonnage.
4. **Foreign WTIV deployment calendar** — Cadeler, Van Oord, Jan De Nul fleet schedules. Industry trackers (4C Offshore, WindEurope). Heavy 2026–2028 European deployment competes with US for vessel time.
5. **CBP penalty notices** under 46 U.S.C. §55102 published or reported in trade press.

### Quantification

A 12-month delay in WTIV availability for a $4B project: **5–10% of capex** in carrying costs and PPA delay LDs. A CBP enforcement action retroactively voiding the feeder model mid-installation (low-probability, high-severity): **10–25% of capex**, plus penalty exposure equal to merchandise value (potentially $100M+).

**Capex incidence of vessel scarcity in normal operating mode:** WTIV day-rate premium (current spot ~$250k/day vs. historical ~$150k/day) adds roughly **1–2% of capex** for a typical 12–18 month installation campaign.

**Probability over 18 months:**
- *Charybdis remains the only operational Jones-Act-compliant WTIV:* **80–90%** — no other US-build is close to delivery
- *CBP issues a ruling tightening feeder-vessel model:* **15–25%**
- *Actual Jones Act enforcement action against an active project:* **5–10%**
- *Charybdis schedule slip materially impacting CVOW or another project:* **20–35%**

## Scenario 4 — IRA §45Y PTC and §48E ITC Durability for Offshore Wind

### Mechanism

The Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 (P.L. 117-169) replaced the legacy §45 PTC and §48 ITC with technology-neutral successors:

- **§45Y Clean Electricity Production Credit** — 26 U.S.C. §45Y. 10-year PTC at base 0.3¢/kWh (×5 = 1.5¢ if prevailing-wage/apprenticeship met, indexed). Applies to facilities placed in service after Dec 31, 2024.
- **§48E Clean Electricity Investment Credit** — 26 U.S.C. §48E. 6% base / 30% with PWA. Domestic content bonus +10 percentage points, energy-community bonus +10 pp.
- **Phase-out**: credits begin to phase down the later of 2032 or the year after annual US power-sector GHG emissions fall ≥75% below 2022 levels. For offshore wind reaching commercial operations in 2027–2030, phase-out is **not the binding risk** — repeal or regulatory narrowing is.

**Domestic content bonus** mechanics for offshore wind: Treasury Notice 2023-38 (May 2023) and Notice 2024-41 (May 2024) set the framework. Offshore wind-specific component classification table in Notice 2024-41 Appendix — monopiles, transition pieces, towers, blades, nacelles, inter-array cable, export cable each have assigned manufactured-product/steel-iron classifications. Threshold: 40% for projects beginning construction before 2025, stepping up to 55% by 2027.

**Transferability** — §6418 — allows one-time cash sale of credits to unrelated taxpayer. Has been the primary monetization channel for offshore wind tax equity post-IRA.

### Current status as of late May 2026

**[VERIFIED through early 2025; INFERRED 2025–2026]**

- **One Big Beautiful Bill Act / FY2026 Reconciliation:** Republican-controlled Congress in 2025 pursued narrowing of IRA energy credits. The author's confident KB data ends with the House Ways & Means draft in May 2025 that proposed (a) accelerating phase-out for wind/solar to facilities beginning construction within 60 days of enactment, and (b) restricting transferability to certain "non-prohibited foreign entities." **Final enacted form as of May 2026: [UNKNOWN with high importance]**. The most-likely landing zone based on the historical pattern of reconciliation negotiation:
  - §45Y/§48E retained for projects that *began construction* by a date in 2025 or early 2026
  - **Offshore wind specifically** treated harshly relative to onshore wind/solar due to perceived alignment with European supply chain
  - Transferability narrowed but not eliminated
  - Foreign Entity of Concern (FEOC) restrictions tightened, hitting Chinese-content cables and electrical equipment
- **"Beginning of construction" safe harbor** under IRS Notice 2018-59 (carried over to §45Y/§48E via Notice 2025-08 or successor): 5% safe-harbor or physical-work test. Offshore wind developers have been aggressively safe-harboring monopile fabrication contracts in 2024–2025 to lock in pre-cutoff status.
- **Treasury offshore-specific guidance:** **Notice 2024-41** is the principal authority on domestic content for offshore wind. **[VERIFIED]** Treasury has not, to KB cutoff, issued offshore-specific final regulations (the May 2024 proposed regs under §45Y/§48E from Treasury REG-119283-23 / REG-132569-17 line are still in proposed form). A finalization or withdrawal under Trump 2.0 Treasury is a high-information event.
- **Contract library milestone "Sec 48E Offshore Domestic Content Ruling"** — corresponds to the Treasury finalization or amendment of the offshore-specific table. As of late May 2026 the author cannot verify status.

### Historical precedent

- **PTC lapse-and-extend cycles 1999–2015:** PTC expired and was retroactively restored five times. Demonstrates that even when credits "expire," reinstatement is common, but mid-development uncertainty is corrosive.
- **2017 TCJA "BEAT" exposure on tax equity:** Created a one-time scramble in tax-equity markets. Resolved by Treasury guidance.
- **§1603 cash grant (2009–2011):** Direct-pay alternative; expired. Direct-pay under IRA §6417 is limited to tax-exempt entities for most credits.

### Leading indicators

1. **Treasury / IRS guidance docket on §45Y/§48E**: https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/businesses/clean-energy-tax-incentives and https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/inflation-reduction-act. Watch for finalization of REG-119283-23 (proposed §45Y/§48E regs, May 2024).
2. **Congressional reconciliation text and JCT scoring**: https://www.congress.gov and https://www.jct.gov. JCT revenue tables for "modifications to §45Y, §48E" line items are the dispositive document.
3. **Tax-equity transfer market pricing**: trade press surveys (Norton Rose Fulbright, Mintz). Offshore-specific transfer discount to onshore wind is the cleanest single proxy for credit risk perception. **Reference range: onshore wind ITC transfers ~92–94¢ on the dollar pre-2025; offshore was ~88–91¢. A widening spread signals stress.**
4. **Beginning-of-construction safe-harbor disclosures** in developer 10-Ks (Avangrid, Eversource [legacy], Dominion, Equinor ASA).
5. **FERC Form 556 / EIA-860 monthly filings** showing offshore wind capacity reaching commercial operation: https://www.eia.gov/electricity/data/eia860/. Pace of COD signals which projects locked in credits.

### Quantification

For a $4B offshore wind project (assume ~1,200 MW, ~38% capacity factor, 30-year life):

- **§48E ITC at 30% (PWA met):** ~$1.2B nominal credit value. After transferability discount (~10–12%) and time-value: ~$1.0B PV.
- **§48E + 10pp domestic content bonus = 40%:** ~$1.6B nominal, ~$1.35B PV.
- **§45Y PTC alternative:** ~10-year stream of 1.5¢/kWh × ~4 TWh/yr × 10 yr ≈ $600M nominal, PV ~$400–450M. Offshore wind typically chooses §48E because of high upfront capex.

**Impact scenarios:**
- *Full repeal applied retroactively to projects not yet placed in service:* **25–35% of capex** — catastrophic, likely litigated under Takings/due process theory
- *Phase-out accelerated to require COD by 2027:* **5–15% of capex** depending on individual project schedule
- *Domestic content bonus narrowed (10pp loss):* **2.5% of capex**
- *Transferability eliminated:* **1.5–3% of capex** (forces traditional tax equity, costlier)
- *FEOC restrictions disqualify specific suppliers:* **0.5–2% of capex** in resourcing

**Probability over 18 months:**
- *Material narrowing of §45Y/§48E applicability to offshore wind (any of: accelerated BOC cutoff, FEOC tightening, transferability narrowing):* **65–80%**
- *Full retroactive repeal:* **5–10%**
- *Treasury finalizes offshore domestic-content table favorably to industry:* **15–25%**
- *Treasury finalizes unfavorably or withdraws Notice 2024-41:* **35–50%**

## Scenario 5 — NEPA / ESA / NMFS Challenges

### Mechanism

Three statutory channels converge on offshore wind:

1. **NEPA (42 U.S.C. §§4321 et seq.)** — requires EIS for major federal actions. Offshore wind COP approval is the principal federal action; BOEM is lead agency. Post-*Seven County Infrastructure* (Supreme Court, 2024) and post-*Loper Bright* (Chevron overruled, 2024), the legal landscape favors challengers in some respects (no agency deference) and defendants in others (narrower scope of effects review). The **Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023** (P.L. 118-5) amended NEPA at §107 to impose page limits and 2-year deadlines, but also clarified judicial review standards. **CEQ Phase 2 regulations (40 CFR 1500-1508)** were largely rescinded by the Trump administration in early 2025 via interim final rule.
2. **ESA §7 (16 U.S.C. §1536)** — consultation requirement. NMFS issues Biological Opinions on impacts to ESA-listed species, most critically the **North Atlantic right whale** (~370 individuals, critically endangered).
3. **MMPA (16 U.S.C. §§1361 et seq.)** — NMFS issues **Incidental Take Authorizations (ITAs)** under §101(a)(5) for both site characterization (geophysical surveys) and construction (pile driving). The **Letter of Authorization (LOA)** is the construction-phase instrument; **Incidental Harassment Authorization (IHA)** is the survey-phase one.

The **Atlantic Shores Clean Air Act EAB remand** is a distinct fourth channel: **EPA's Outer Continental Shelf Air Permit** under CAA §328 (42 U.S.C. §7627). EPA Region 2 issued the OCS air permit for Atlantic Shores South. Petitioners (Save LBI, others) appealed to the **Environmental Appeals Board (EAB)**. **EAB remanded** in 2024 on grounds related to BACT analysis and environmental justice considerations.

### Current status as of late May 2026

**[VERIFIED through 2024–early 2025; INFERRED for late 2025–2026]**

- **Right whale litigation:** Multiple suits filed 2022–2024 by Responsible Offshore Development Alliance (RODA), Nantucket-area groups, and others challenging NMFS BiOps and IHAs for Vineyard Wind, South Fork, Revolution, CVOW. ### Historical precedent

- **Cape Wind v. NMFS / FWS** (D. Mass., D.C. Cir., 2010–2016): multiple NEPA/ESA challenges, mostly defeated, but cumulative delay material.
- **Vineyard Wind 1 NEPA litigation** (*ACK Residents Against Turbines v. BOEM*, D. Mass. 2023; aff'd 1st Cir. 2024): denied. Demonstrated that a well-built NEPA record survives challenge.
- **Lobstermen's Association v. NMFS** (D.D.C. / D.C. Cir., 2022–2023): right whale fishing-gear restrictions — illustrates NMFS exposure to take-authorization litigation on both sides.
- **EAB remand base rate:** EAB remands ~20–30% of contested permits historically; cure on remand is usually feasible within 6–18 months *if the permittee remains motivated*.

### Quantification

NEPA/ESA delay incidence for a $4B project:

- *Standard challenge to ROD, denied at district court:* ~$10–40M legal and carrying costs (≤1% of capex).
- *Preliminary injunction granted pending appeal (rare but live since Trump 2.0 changed NMFS posture):* 6–12 month delay, **3–8% of capex.**
- *Full ROD vacatur with remand for supplemental EIS:* 18–36 month delay, **8–20% of capex.**
- *EAB-style CAA permit remand requiring new BACT analysis:* 12–24 months, **5–12% of capex.**
- *Vessel speed restrictions tightened (right whale 10-knot rule expansion to currently-exempt sizes):* permanent **0.5–1.5% capex** equivalent operating cost.

**Probability over 18 months:**
- *At least one NEPA challenge filed against a major project's COP/ROD:* **70–85%** (base rate)
- *Project enjoined or ROD vacated:* **10–18%**
- *Atlantic Shores CAA permit cured and project reactivated:* **5–15%**
- *NMFS BiOp re-initiation triggered for an operating project (e.g., right whale mortality event tied to wind area):* **15–25%**
